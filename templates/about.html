Technical Architecture

Why Real-World BEV Range Differs from Lab Ratings

Laboratory dynamometer cycles (WLTP, ARAI, EPA) evaluate Battery Electric Vehicles under idealized static ambient conditions. In real road deployments, non-linear physical loads dictate energy dissipation.

Aerodynamic Drag

Aerodynamic power scales cubically with velocity ($P_{\text{aero}} \propto v^3$). Cruising at 120 km/h demands over 2.3× more energy than 80 km/h.

$P_{\text{aero}} = \frac{0.5 \rho C_d A v^3}{1000}$
Thermal HVAC Aux

Sub-zero temperatures double electrolyte internal resistance. Cabin heating draws 2.5 to 4.5 kW directly from the traction pack without engine waste heat.

$P_{\text{hvac}} = 0.5 + 0.09 \cdot |\Delta T|$
Terrain & Incline

Climbing gradients demands continuous potential energy. While regenerative braking recovers ~65-75% downhill, net climb drives substantial loss.

$P_{\text{grade}} = \frac{M g \sin(\theta) v}{1000}$
Traffic Kinetic Losses

Frequent stop-and-go acceleration cycles incur round-trip electrochemical conversion losses and continuous base auxiliary idle draw.

$\eta_{\text{regen}} \approx 68\% - 75\%$

End-to-End Scikit-Learn Pipeline

1. Physics Simulation Dataset 2,800 drive cycles synthesized via validated automotive longitudinal dynamics equations
2. ColumnTransformer StandardScaler for physics floats, OneHotEncoder for categories
3. HistGradientBoosting Histogram-based binned gradient tree regressor (max_iter=100)
4. Prediction Output Remaining km, 90% uncertainty bound, normalized 100% equivalent
